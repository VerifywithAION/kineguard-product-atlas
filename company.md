## 1. Executive summary

**KINEGuard** is an independent, solo-founder project developing security and economic governance for wallets and autonomous agents. Its core thesis is **KEY IS NOT ENOUGH**: possession of a valid signing key should not imply unlimited authority to move value.

**KINEGuard** is the user-facing application; **KINE OS** is the underlying research and governance architecture for identity, permissions, owner authority, policy, economic exposure, decisions and evidence. The long-term business hypothesis is a trust and authorization layer that can integrate with external agent-payment infrastructure without transferring ownership of KINE's internal engine.

**Current boundary:** historical lab/testnet mechanisms and a published documentation product exist. A fully integrated and customer-accepted nine-control production release is **not verified**.

## 2. Customer problem and proposed solution

An AI agent, wallet or automation may possess a key or API credential without legitimately being authorized for every transfer, spender, destination, asset or budget. A simple per-transaction cap does not necessarily prevent cumulative spending, concurrent requests or stale authorization.

KINEGuard proposes an owner-bound control plane with **pre-execution review, scoped permissions, cumulative economic exposure, pending capacity reservations, governance timing and verifiable decision records**. Actual enforcement depends on the supported provider, contract and route. It is not a universal claim to stop every drain or signing attack.

## 4. Target customers and product positioning

**Hypotheses to validate—not existing customer claims:** agent-payment infrastructure providers, wallet/smart-account teams, AI-agent treasury operators, and tokenized-asset protocols needing independent authorization rules. The initial go-to-market approach would be **developer-first B2B**: an integration pilot, evidence-backed security evaluation, and conversion to a recurring product if willingness to pay is observed.

KINEGuard should not be pitched as merely another wallet, another trading agent, or a generic daily spend cap. The proposed differentiation is **independent owner-bound economic authority and evidence across supported execution paths**. No published independent comparative benchmark proves superiority over existing wallet/security stacks.

## 5. Business model — proposals, not revenue

Potential revenue architecture: scoped paid integration pilots, subscription for protected organizations/wallets/agents, transparent usage-based pricing when unit economics warrant it, and enterprise security/support agreements. Consumer tools may support adoption without becoming the main revenue driver.

**No verified numbers are available for** customers, paying pilots, ARR/MRR, conversion, CAC, LTV, churn or gross margin. No such figures are fabricated in this profile. Pricing is a testable hypothesis, not a currently verified commercial tariff.

## 9. Risks, diligence and evidence discipline

Material risks: incomplete production integration, alternative signer routes bypassing pre-sign hooks, loss of reservation consistency, wallet-specific gaps, security liability, incumbent provider controls, unproven distribution and accidental disclosure of the private engine.

Planned mitigation: release-specific verification, fail-closed gates, independent security QA, per-wallet certifications, signed owner policies, reservation reconciliation, measured economics and selective disclosure. Every future metric should carry **date, period, unit, source, environment and status**.
